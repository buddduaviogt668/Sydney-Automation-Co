Hospitality Venue FAQ

Common Questions from
Venue Operators

Do hotels and pubs need an AFSS for emergency lighting? +
Yes. Hotels, pubs, clubs, RSLs and restaurants are all classified as buildings with essential fire safety measures under NSW legislation. They are required to submit an Annual Fire Safety Statement (AFSS) to their local council each year, certifying that emergency lighting has been tested and is operational.
Can a failed exit sign or spitfire prevent us from trading? +
In serious cases, yes. If your emergency lighting is found to be non-compliant during a fire safety inspection, the council or fire brigade can issue orders requiring rectification. Persistent non-compliance can result in fines or orders affecting your ability to operate. We recommend addressing any known faults immediately.
Do you work for hospitality venues? +
Yes. We understand that hospitality venues operate during business hours and may need emergency lighting work done outside trading times. We can arrange early morning, late evening, or attendance to minimise disruption to your operations.
We have a RAPIX Emergency system — can you work on it? +
Yes. Sydney Automation Co. are RAPIX specialists. We can commission, fault-find, repair, and maintain RAPIX Emergency systems in hotels, clubs, RSLs and all hospitality venues. We carry the software and have the experience to resolve RAPIX faults that general Automation Specialists cannot.
Can you work alongside our existing Automation Specialist? +
Absolutely. We regularly work alongside venue Automation Specialists, providing specialist RAPIX and emergency lighting expertise while their team handles general electrical work. We can also bring our own licensed Automation Specialists if required.
How much does emergency lighting compliance cost for a hospitality venue? +
Cost depends on the size of your venue, the number of emergency lights, and the extent of any faults. We provide a free assessment and quote before any work begins. Call 0422 469 739 to discuss your venue and get an indication of cost.

What Happened to Your C-Bus System During the Storm?

When a storm hits Sydney, power lines experience high-voltage spikes, phase interruptions, and lightning-induced voltage surges. Clipsal C-Bus operates on an extra-low voltage (36V DC) pink communications bus. Here is what your symptoms mean:

⚡

Switches Flashing Rhythmically

Are your Saturn, Neo, or DLT wall switches continuously flashing all their LED indicators together?

What it means: The network has lost its master clock or system burden, or the bus voltage has dropped below the critical operating threshold (typically below 20V DC) due to a damaged power supply module.

📴

C-Bus Indicators Totally Gone

Are your wall keypads completely unlit, with no indicators, and the buttons do nothing when pressed?

What it means: The dedicated C-Bus 350mA power supply (5500PS) or the internal power supply within a DIN-rail relay has sustained a surge and failed completely. The pink data cable has 0 volts.

🔌

Lights Stuck ON or Stuck OFF

Certain circuits won't turn off, or entire banks of lights refuse to respond, even though other areas work?

What it means: Storm surges can weld mechanical relay contacts shut (leaving lights stuck ON) or damage the microprocessor on individual channel boards in your distribution board.

💡

Important Safety Advice During or Immediately After Bad Weather

Avoid repeatedly toggling safety switches or main circuit breakers if there is an audible hum or burning smell coming from the lighting automation distribution board. Leave the affected breakers off and call an accredited specialist.

Why are my C-Bus switches flashing rapidly after the storm? +
When all LED indicators on C-Bus keypads (Saturn, Neo, DLT) flash in sync, it indicates the unit cannot detect a stable 36V DC bus voltage, or the system network clock has failed. This is the classic symptom of a blown standalone power supply (5500PS) or a damaged power supply built into an older relay module. Once the voltage is restored, the switches stop flashing and work normally.
Can a storm erase my C-Bus programming? +
Generally, no. C-Bus units store their programming parameters in non-volatile flash memory (EEPROM), meaning the logic and group addresses are not lost simply because the power failed. However, if a module suffers severe physical surge damage, that unit's internal board may be damaged. We can scan the remaining network, reconstruct the database using C-Bus Toolkit, and reprogram new replacement units to match your existing setup.

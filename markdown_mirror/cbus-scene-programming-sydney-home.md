C-Bus Scene Programming: How to Get the Most Out of Your Sydney Home
====================================================================

Most C-Bus homes use less than 20% of what their system can do. Here's how to set up scenes that actually make life better \u2014 not just turn lights on.

[C-Bus](https://www.se.com/au/en/work/products/product-launch/cbus/) is one of Australia's most capable home automation platforms. Most homeowners in Sydney \u2014 from Cronulla to Mosman, from Caringbah to Paddington \u2014 have a C-Bus system installed by their builder that does the basics and not much more. The scenes were set up by a sparky who knew enough to commission the system but not enough to make it sing.

Here's how George approaches scene programming for Sydney homeowners who want more from their system.

**Start With How You Actually Live**

The best C-Bus programming starts with a conversation, not a laptop. George always asks: when do you wake up? What's the first thing you do? When do you entertain? What annoys you about how the system works now? The answers drive everything. Generic scenes \u2014 "Morning," "Evening," "Away" \u2014 are a starting point, not an endpoint.

**The 5 Scenes Every Sydney Home Should Have**

Morning ramp-up: Lights come on gradually in the kitchen and bathroom starting at your wake time, reaching full brightness over 20 minutes. No harsh lights at 6am.

Movie/entertain: Living area at 15% warm, kitchen at 30%, all other areas off. Single button press from the couch.

Dinner: Dining at 60\u201370% warm, kitchen work areas bright, living dim. Makes the table the focus.

All-off check: One button press from the bedroom confirms every light in the house is off. Eliminates the "did I leave the kitchen lights on" problem entirely.

Away: Randomised on/off cycles across multiple circuits when you're not home, simulating occupancy. Proper deterrent, not a timer.

**Schedules That Earn Their Keep**

The most appreciated C-Bus feature in Sydney homes isn't scenes \u2014 it's schedules. Outdoor lights on at sunset, off at midnight. Sensor-triggered pathway lighting when someone comes home late. Guest room lights off automatically after 11pm. These automations feel invisible when they work and are missed immediately when they don't.

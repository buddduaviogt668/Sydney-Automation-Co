Understanding Your Compliance Requirements

AFSS, OC & PC — What Each One Means
-----------------------------------

Three different compliance milestones. All require your emergency lighting to be tested, operational, and documented. Here's what each one means for your building.

AFSS

### Annual Fire Safety Statement

Required every year by NSW councils for all buildings with essential fire safety measures. Your fire safety practitioner must certify that emergency lighting has been tested and is operational. A single failed spitfire or dead exit sign can hold up your entire AFSS.

**Who needs it:** All commercial buildings, strata complexes, hotels, clubs, pubs, RSLs, restaurants, shopping centres, offices

OC

### Occupation Certificate

Required at the end of a construction or major renovation project before a building can be legally occupied. All essential services — including emergency lighting — must be tested, commissioned and certified. Developers and builders cannot hand over a building without a valid OC.

**Who needs it:** Developers, builders, project managers, building owners completing new builds or major refurbishments

PC

### Practical Completion

The contractual milestone at which a construction project is considered substantially complete. Emergency lighting must be fully installed, tested and operational before PC can be signed off. Delays in emergency lighting compliance can hold up the entire project handover.

**Who needs it:** Builders, head contractors, project managers, fitout contractors

Common Compliance Failures

Why Buildings Fail Emergency Lighting Inspections
-------------------------------------------------

Most emergency lighting compliance failures are preventable. They come down to ageing batteries, failed luminaires, or systems that have never been properly commissioned. Our inspection process identifies every fault before your certifier does.

We work to AS2293 — the Australian Standard for emergency lighting — and provide written test records that your fire safety practitioner can use to certify your AFSS or support your OC/PC application.

Compliance FAQ

Your Questions Answered
-----------------------

What is an AFSS and why does emergency lighting matter? +

An Annual Fire Safety Statement (AFSS) is a document that building owners in NSW must submit to their local council each year, confirming that all essential fire safety measures — including emergency lighting — are maintained and operational. If your emergency lighting fails testing, your AFSS cannot be issued.

What happens if my building fails the AFSS emergency lighting inspection? +

If your emergency lighting fails inspection, your fire safety practitioner cannot certify your AFSS. This can result in council notices, fines, and in serious cases, orders to vacate the building. It is essential to identify and repair all faults before your AFSS assessment.

How long does the 90-minute emergency lighting test take? +

The AS2293 standard requires emergency lighting to operate for a minimum of 90 minutes on battery backup. The actual discharge test takes 90 minutes plus preparation time. We conduct these tests on-site and provide written records of all results.

Can you fix emergency lighting faults before my AFSS inspection? +

Yes. We specialise in identifying and repairing emergency lighting faults under time pressure. We carry common replacement parts and can often complete repairs on the same visit. For larger upgrade works, we provide a fast-tracked quote and schedule.

Do you provide the documentation my fire safety practitioner needs? +

Yes. We provide full test records, fault reports, and completion certificates in the format required by your fire safety practitioner. Our documentation is designed to support your AFSS certification, OC application, or PC sign-off.

What Australian Standard applies to emergency lighting? +

Emergency lighting in Australia is governed by AS2293, which sets requirements for the design, installation, and testing of emergency lighting systems. We conduct all testing and documentation to AS2293 standards.
